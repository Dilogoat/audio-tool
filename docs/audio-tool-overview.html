What the tool does

Overview

Editing a one-hour interview, audiobook chapter, or voiceover by ear means scrubbing for the same handful of recurring problems over and over. This tool does that pass for you. It analyses a recording offline, marks every region that an editor would normally hunt for, and lets you audition a cleaned version before committing anything to disk. You stay in control: nothing is altered destructively, and every decision is exposed as a tunable threshold.

Hear less, edit faster

Three detectors run in parallel and place colour-coded markers directly on the waveform, so the work is to review — not to find.

Decisions stay yours

Every threshold is a live slider. The tool proposes; you confirm. Markers and cleaned audio are only written when you export them.

Fits your pipeline

Markers export to plain JSON for use in other tools, and cleanup is rendered to a standard WAV you can drop straight into a session.

How each detector listens

The audio reasoning

No black boxes. Each detector uses a transparent, physically intuitive measurement, with defaults tuned for spoken word. The values below are the shipping defaults — all are adjustable live while you watch the markers update.

Pause

RMS energy gate

The signal is split into short overlapping windows (~50 ms) and the RMS level of each is measured to form an energy envelope. Any stretch that stays below the loudness threshold for longer than the minimum duration is marked as a pause — the same judgement you make when you see a flat patch on the waveform and decide it is silence, not signal.

Defaults: RMS threshold ≈ 0.01 (linear) · minimum duration 0.5 s. Lower the threshold for quiet rooms; raise the duration to ignore natural beats between sentences.

Click & plosive

Transient spike ratio

Using very short windows (~5 ms), each frame's energy is compared against the average of its immediate neighbours. When a single frame leaps well above its surroundings — and is loud enough in absolute terms to matter — it is flagged as a transient. This is how a mouth click or a hard "P" reveals itself: a momentary stab of energy that the surrounding speech does not explain.

Defaults: spike ratio ≥ 4× local average · minimum level 0.005 · marked span ≈ 30 ms. The marker's score scales with how far the spike exceeds the threshold.

Breath

Spectral tilt + low level

A breath is quiet but bright. Each spectral frame is divided into a low band and a high band; when most of the energy sits in the upper two-thirds of the spectrum and the overall level is below the speech floor, the moment looks like an inhale rather than a voiced sound. Runs that fall within a plausible breath length are marked.

Defaults: high-frequency ratio > 0.55 · speech floor ≈ 0.03 RMS · duration window 0.08 s – 1.5 s · analysis FFT 2048 / hop 512.

Why thresholds, not magic. Heuristic detectors are predictable and explainable: if a marker looks wrong, you can see exactly which measurement triggered it and nudge that one control. A machine-learning path is scaffolded for the future (see Section 7) but is not required for day-to-day use.

The composite score

Prioritising your time

Not every flagged event deserves equal attention. The composite score collapses all markers into one normalised curve along the timeline, weighting each event type by how disruptive it typically is to a listener. The result is a heatmap that tells you where to look first.

Clicks — weight 0.8

The most jarring artifact for a listener, so it dominates the score wherever it appears.

Breaths — weight 0.5

Often acceptable, sometimes distracting — a middle priority you may keep or soften.

Pauses — weight 0.2

Rarely an error, usually a pacing choice; contributes gently to the overall curve.

Scores accumulate into 50 ms frames and are normalised to a 0–1 peak, so the heatmap is relative to the busiest moment in your recording — not an absolute scale.

Cleanup preview & export

Non-destructive

The cleanup stage works in the frequency domain. Audio is transformed with a 2048-point STFT (512 hop); inside the regions marked as clicks or breaths, the spectral content is attenuated by a configurable factor and the signal is resynthesised. Pauses are not touched by cleanup — they are informational markers, not noise to remove.

Attenuate, don't delete

By default marked regions are reduced to ~15% of their level rather than silenced outright, preserving timing and avoiding hard cuts. Push it toward zero for full removal, or up for a gentle touch.

Audition first

Built-in playback lets you hear the recording with markers in context before exporting. The original audio is always preserved; the cleaned version is rendered separately.

Set expectations: spectral attenuation is a fast, broad-strokes cleanup ideal for taming breaths and softening clicks across a long file. It is not a surgical spectral-repair brush — for forensic, single-artifact restoration you would still reach for a dedicated repair tool.

A typical session

Workflow

Open the recording

Load a WAV, FLAC, OGG, or AIFF file. The waveform, duration, and sample rate appear immediately.

Analyse

Run all three detectors in one pass. Markers, the RMS envelope, the composite heatmap, and the spectrogram populate together.

Review against the heatmap

Follow the composite score to the busiest moments first. Scrub, play, and confirm each flagged event in context.

Tune the thresholds

Adjust pause level, click sensitivity, breath brightness, or cleanup strength with live sliders and watch markers update.

Export

Write markers to JSON for use elsewhere, and/or render a cleaned mono WAV. Your source file is never modified.

Where it's heading

Roadmap

The detectors today are heuristic and transparent. A machine-learning pathway is already scaffolded: a neural model (a convolutional + recurrent network over spectrogram slices) would classify each moment as speech, breath, click, or silence with greater nuance than fixed thresholds. It is gated on building a labelled dataset of real voice sessions and will arrive as an optional mode — the heuristic detectors remain the dependable default.

Marker export format

Interoperability

Markers export as a flat, human-readable JSON array — easy to parse, diff, or feed into another application. Each entry carries its type, start and end time in seconds, and a confidence score.

  "markers": [
    { "type": "pause",  "start_sec": 12.40, "end_sec": 13.65, "score": 1.00 },

    { "type": "breath", "start_sec": 24.05, "end_sec": 24.38, "score": 1.00 }
  ]

Good to know

Scope
  • Runs natively on Windows 10/11 as a desktop application.
  • Imports WAV, FLAC, OGG, and AIFF; cleaned audio is rendered to mono WAV.
  • Analysis runs offline on the whole file — no internet or cloud upload.
  • Source audio is never modified in place; exports are always new files.
  • Tuned for spoken-word material; music and dense mixes are out of scope.
  • Cleanup is broad spectral attenuation, not surgical spectral repair.
  • Detection is heuristic today; an optional ML mode is on the roadmap.
  • Best results come from reviewing markers, not exporting blind.
